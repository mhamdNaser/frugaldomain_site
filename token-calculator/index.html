Getting the exact count

A free endpoint that runs the model's own tokeniser and charges nothing for the call.

import anthropic

client = anthropic.Anthropic()

count = client.messages.count_tokens(
    model="claude-opus-5",
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": text}],
)

print(count.input_tokens)

Why an estimate and not a count

A real tokeniser means shipping a vocabulary of tens of thousands of entries - and the wrong one for whichever model you use, since every provider tokenises differently. This page estimates from the character mix instead, and tells you its margin.

Why Arabic costs more

Most tokeniser vocabularies were built mainly on English text, so Arabic words break into several pieces and diacritics often cost a token each. The same paragraph can cost fifty per cent more in Arabic than in English - this page accounts for that.

Where the prices come from

Each provider's own published page, read on 2026-09-20 and linked above. Tiered rates, promotional rates and off-peak discounts are noted on the row they apply to. Cloud marketplaces such as Bedrock and Vertex are priced separately and are not modelled here.

Frequently asked questions

How many tokens is my prompt?
Paste it in and the estimate appears as you type, along with the margin it is accurate to. Roughly, English prose runs about four characters to the token, so a thousand characters is around 250 tokens - but code, punctuation-heavy text and Arabic all break that ratio, which is why the page measures the character mix rather than dividing by four.
Why is this an estimate rather than an exact token count?
An exact count needs the tokeniser vocabulary of the specific model, tens of thousands of entries, and every provider uses a different one. Shipping all of them into a browser tab would cost more to download than the page itself. The estimate reads the character mix instead and states its margin, which is enough to choose a model or to budget a feature.
Why does Arabic cost more tokens than English?
Tokeniser vocabularies were built mostly on English text, so Arabic words break into several pieces and diacritics often cost a token each. The same paragraph can run about fifty per cent more expensive in Arabic than in English. This page counts Arabic separately rather than applying an English ratio to it.
Are the prices up to date?
Every price on the page carries the date it was last checked and links to the provider's own pricing page. Model prices change without notice, so treat the figure as correct as of that date and follow the link before committing to a budget.
